Related literature

What earlier studies found, and the gap this study fills.

Foreign literature

StudyWhat it contributes
Chandola, Banerjee & Kumar (2009)Surveyed anomaly detection techniques, including intrusion detection, and noted that a main advantage is flagging previously unseen events.
Ring et al. (2019)Reviewed network-based intrusion datasets and stressed realistic, well-labeled traffic for training and evaluation.
Kolias et al. (2016)Evaluated threats to 802.11 networks and released the AWID dataset, a common Wi-Fi benchmark.
Sharafaldin et al. (2018)Produced CICIDS2017, with benign traffic alongside common modern attacks.
Ahmad et al. (2021)Systematic study of machine learning and deep learning approaches to network intrusion detection.
Liu, Ting & Zhou (2008)Introduced Isolation Forest, an efficient unsupervised algorithm that isolates anomalies.
Mirsky et al. (2018)Proposed Kitsune, an ensemble of autoencoders for online intrusion detection on low-cost devices.
Doriguzzi-Corin et al. (2020)Presented LUCID, a lightweight deep learning solution for DDoS detection.

Local literature

StudyWhat it contributes
Natonton (2018)Examined cybersecurity awareness in Butuan City schools; public and private institutions differed in having a unit responsible for network security.
De Ramos & Esponilla (2022)Interviewed IT officers of state universities and colleges in the NCR: key challenges were user education, cloud security and unsecured personal devices, none had a dedicated cybersecurity position, and budgets were limited.
Aquino & Noroña (2021)Argued that signature-based tools are reactive and proposed AI that learns a network's normal behavior and flags anomalies.
Miranda, Tayag & Canlas (2025)Found a shortage of skilled cybersecurity professionals, supporting the need for automated tools small IT teams can run.

Synthesis

Foreign studies show that unsupervised and semi-supervised learning can detect unknown threats, that lightweight models can run on low-cost hardware, and that suitable datasets exist for evaluation. Local studies show that Philippine campuses and similar organizations depend on limited budgets and small IT staff, and that unauthorized access and abnormal device behavior are real concerns.

The gap. Few of the studies reviewed describe a practical, near real-time anomaly-based IDS with an administrator-facing dashboard for small LANs in the Philippine setting. This study addresses that gap.
